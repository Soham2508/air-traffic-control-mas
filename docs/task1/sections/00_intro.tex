\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Air traffic control (ATC) keeps aircraft safely apart. Two aircraft are in \emph{conflict} when, at the same moment, they are predicted to be within the separation minima used in this work: 5\,NM horizontally \emph{and} 1000\,ft vertically~\cite{icao4444}. Tactical ATC consists of three steps: detecting such conflicts early, assessing how serious they are, and choosing a manoeuvre that removes them without creating new ones~\cite{kuchar2000}.

The aim of this work is to design a multi-agent system (MAS) that performs this task on a simulated airspace and is implemented with the CrewAI framework~\cite{crewai}. The input is an airspace graph, a set of aircraft and their flight plans. From these, the system must:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item validate and normalise the input data;
    \item detect every loss of separation and characterise it;
    \item propose resolution manoeuvres and verify that they are safe;
    \item produce a Markdown ATC report with the final clearance status of every flight.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Why a multi-agent system?} The problem splits naturally into distinct kinds of expertise, much as a real control centre does: data handling, surveillance, tactical planning, independent safety checking and reporting. Each of these is assigned to a crew of specialised agents that cooperate through a shared flow. Collaborative agents are suited to problems that are physically distributed and need several specialists to cooperate, and air-traffic control is one of the standard examples~\cite{urvlect3}; multi-agent formulations of ATC conflict resolution also appear in the research literature~\cite{tomlin1998}. Our design also keeps the agent that plans a manoeuvre separate from the agent that certifies it, a separation of duties that is common in safety-critical operations.

\textbf{Scope and assumptions.} The system is an academic simulation, not an operational tool. Thus, we make the following simplifying assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{Batch processing.} All information is available at the start. The system analyses it, reports and terminates; no new information arrives during execution.
    \item \emph{Simplified kinematics.} Aircraft fly straight legs between waypoints at constant speed and at their cruise altitude. Wind is not modelled.
    \item \emph{No real clearances.} Statuses such as \textsc{cleared} are outputs of the simulation, not instructions to pilots.
    \item \emph{Design over output quality.} The quality of the language-model output depends on the LLM used. The design therefore assigns every safety-relevant computation to deterministic tools rather than to the agents' own reasoning.
\end{itemize}
